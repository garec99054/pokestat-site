Do the value flags predict forward returns?

Cards flagged undervalued, overvalued, or fair are tracked forward about 90 days, after both the Wikipedia pageview and character-premium look-aheads were reconstructed as-of each snapshot date.

Cards flagged cheap, expensive or fairly priced are followed for about 90 days to see what actually happened next. Both inputs that could have peeked at the future, Wikipedia page views and the character popularity premium, were rebuilt to use only what was known on each snapshot date. The cheap cards did not reliably beat the expensive ones: the gap flips sign as the market changes, so it is not something you can trade.

Value-flag backtest

Cards flagged undervalued, overvalued, or fair are tracked forward about 90 days across 8 snapshots. The headline is the undervalued-minus-overvalued mean-return spread of +1.0 points, which is positive: so undervalued flags modestly outperformed overvalued ones.

This spread is small and has been regime-dependent (negative in 2025, positive in 2026), and the leakage-free as-of-T desirability inputs (Wikipedia pageviews and character premium rebuilt at each date) leave no look-ahead lift, so it should be read as a weak, unstable signal rather than a durable edge.

The per-flag groups are also small and share recurring cards, so the remaining differences are best read as largely noise.

What changed when look-aheads were removed

Both the Wikipedia pageview appeal input and the character premium input are now reconstructed as of each snapshot date T, using only information a trader at T could have had. Under the earlier look-ahead-biased desirability input the undervalued flag showed mechanical outperformance; with the leakage removed that edge is gone.
