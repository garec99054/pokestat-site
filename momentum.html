Momentum and short-horizon reversion

Significance uses an overlap-deflated effective sample size, not the naive fold count.

Each rebalance the universe is sorted into quintiles by trailing formation return and held three months. A positive winners-minus-losers spread would mean momentum; a negative one means reversion.

Short horizons: past 1-month winners underperformed losers by 3.0 points (t_eff -1.8, null) and past 3-month winners by 6.4 points (t_eff -1.7, null). Neither clears the overlap-deflated significance line, so these are point estimates inside the noise band, not measured effects.

Practically, there is nothing here to trade on in either direction: the pooled data does not support chasing recent winners or fading them.

The same cards, ranked inside their own era

The table above sorts all modeled cards into quintiles against each other, so a card is partly sorted on which era it belongs to rather than on its return against its own peers. The blocked estimator changes exactly one thing: quintiles are formed within each era and the per-era spreads are averaged. Same universe, same rebalance dates, same overlap-deflated significance machinery.

A pooled null has two very different explanations: no effect anywhere, or an effect inside each stratum that pooling washes out. Pooling eras that sit in different lifecycle phases means the pooled quintile sort partly sorts cards on era membership rather than on their return relative to their own peers.

At the 1-month window the pooled spread is -3.0 points (t_eff -1.77, no signal); ranking the same cards inside their own era gives -5.4 points (t_eff -5.44, reversion). Nothing about the cards, the dates or the significance machinery changed, only which field each card is ranked against.

Read the verdict words carefully. no signal means a measurable spread indistinguishable from zero. insufficient means there was not enough independent data to distinguish anything at all, which is a different statement and is never counted as a null.

Not independent of the value backtest. The value backtest tags a card "overvalued" when its market price sits well above the forward model's fair value, and those overvalued cards are drawn disproportionately from this study's top (past-winner) quintile, markedly so at the longer 6-month formation window, because a recent run-up is exactly what lifts market above fair value. So the two studies re-test overlapping recent-run-up cards rather than independent samples. Neither shows a significant pooled effect: the pooled quintile spreads here are within noise once the overlapping holds are deflated, and the backtest's under-minus-over spread is small and regime-dependent. Read them as two lenses on the same population, not as independent confirmations.
