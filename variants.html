What is the variant premium today

Some cards print in two or more finishes, such as a Reverse Holofoil next to a plain Normal copy of the same card. This page measures how much more the fancier finish costs on the same day, as a multiple and in dollars. It is a snapshot, not a trend.

Technical wording

For every card printed in more than one TCGplayer variant, the multiplier and dollar gap between the premium finish and the plainer finish, measured from the same day's market prices.

Experimental: these rankings come from models that have not passed a forward test yet. Each month's predictions are saved before prices move and scored after three months. Not a recommendation. Any model return shown here is before costs. Bought at a realistic price and sold after fees, the top 10% returned -22.9% over 3 months against -37.3% for all cards. It beat the average card in 7 of 8 months and made money in 0 of 8. Fixed fees and shipping swamp cheap cards: among cards $20 and up, the top 10% returned -3.7% against -15.0%, beat them in 8 of 8 months and made money in 4 of 8. The months overlap, so these are not independent results.

Variant premium

5.2xReverse Holofoil prices at 5.2x Normal, median over 2,532 pairs with a base of $1 or more.
1.7xHolofoil prices at 1.7x Normal, median over 142 pairs with a base of $1 or more.
2.8x1st Edition prices at 2.8x Unlimited, median over 615 pairs with a base of $1 or more.
2.6x1st Edition Holofoil prices at 2.6x Unlimited Holofoil, median over 164 pairs with a base of $1 or more.

A pair forms only when both finishes are priced on the SAME product on the SAME day; the premium finish is the numerator and the more common finish is the denominator, so a multiplier above 1x means the premium finish cost more that day. A card with no TCGplayer product mapping still gets a row here, shown by its product name rather than a linked card cell. Per-set medians are shown only where a set has at least 8 same-day pairs for that pairing, so a thin set does not produce a noisy number.

Read this before the table. These are same-day TCGplayer prices, a measurement, not a call. A base price under a dollar makes the multiplier unreliable, and those rows are excluded from the table below. The price history behind this page is short (daily since 2026-07-22), so no trend is claimed here, only the day's snapshot.

Showing the 600 widest dollar gaps of 3453 non-penny pairs; 8889 pairs with a base under a dollar are left out; the complete table is in variant_premium_daily.csv.
