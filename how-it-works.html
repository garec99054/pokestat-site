How the models work

How the numbers on this site are made, drawn as labelled diagrams that match the actual code: where the prices come from, how a fair value is estimated from a card's own attributes, how the 90-day forecast and the range around it are built, and when a card gets flagged. One card is traced the whole way through, and the last section is an honest account of what these numbers can and cannot tell you.

Technical wording

The data pipeline, the hedonic and forward models, the conformal band and the dual-flag rule, as labelled diagrams grounded in the actual code, with one card traced end to end and an honest account of what the numbers can and cannot tell you.

Every number on this site traces back to one leakage-free pipeline. This page shows each stage as a diagram and walks through the code that builds it, then traces a single card, the top up-flag, from raw price to final call. The governing rule throughout: a model asked to predict what happens after date T may only ever read data dated on or before T.

1 · Data pipeline, as of T

Four external feeds land in four tables: daily TCGplayer prices become price_history (per card, the MAX market over that card's products via card_product_map); Cardmarket EUR is stored display-only; Wikimedia pageviews and the pull-rate / artwork / sealed feeds fill the feature_* tables. panel.build_panel joins them into a monthly (card, T) panel where every predictor (momentum, volatility, log_price, mispricing_resid, premium_asof, set age, appeal and sealed trends) is reconstructed strictly from same-date-or-earlier data.

The same daily feed carries four price points the panel used to discard at aggregation time: lowPrice, midPrice, highPrice and directLowPrice alongside marketPrice. features.spread now turns them into 6 scale-free as-of-T features describing the shape of the listing book around the traded price: rel_spread, market_pos, mid_skew, direct_discount, d_rel_spread_3m, d_market_pos_3m. They are read from the date-T snapshot (and T−3m for the two deltas) and are populated across all 31 historical monthly snapshots, so they are genuine history rather than a present-day snapshot leaking backwards.

They raised the held-out IC, and the Models page states plainly what that gain mostly is: nearly all of it is one feature, mid_skew, whose univariate IC decays as the horizon lengthens. That is the signature of TCGplayer's trailing market average catching up to a listing book already visible at T, leakage-free, but substantially a measurement lag rather than a forecast of demand. See the full disclosure.

panel.zscore_by_date standardises features cross-sectionally within each date only (never pooling the future distribution into the past), and panel.select_training admits a training row to a fold only once its forward window has fully closed (fwd_end ≤ T). That is the as-of-T boundary drawn in accent below.

2 · Desirability / hedonic model

One feature deserves its own model. panel.compute_mispricing fits, at each date T, a hedonic cross-section log(price_T) ~ log(pull_cost_packs) + desirability (regress.fit_model, OLS), where desirability is built from the as-of-T character premium (backtest.attach_asof_premium) and as-of-T appeal (backtest.attach_asof_appeal): the static 2026 snapshots are deliberately excluded so no future price leaks in.

The externally studentized residual answers one question: is this card dearer or cheaper than its own attributes imply? That single residual enters the forward panel as mispricing_resid, the model's only “value” signal.

3 · Walk-forward folds

fit.walk_forward drives an expanding-window backtest over the panel's monthly panel dates. outlook.split_folds divides the test months into an earlier selection block and a later, disjoint evaluation block. For each test month T the model trains on all rows whose 3-month target has already realised, predicts the cross-section at T, then waits the forward gap (FORWARD_MONTHS = 3) for the honest target y = log(P₍T+3m₎ / P_T), taken market-relative.

outlook.choose_best picks the winning model on the selection folds; it is then graded only on the held-out evaluation folds. fit.model_fold_predictions computes each fold once so the walk-forward summary and the conformal calibration reuse identical fits.

The set-age lifecycle, seen directly

Here is the whole story in one picture. Each dot is a modeled set placed at its current age (months since release) against the median 3-month return the model predicts for its chase cards (accent, sized by card count), with the return those cards have actually realized over the trailing three months behind it (hollow). The accent fit line is the model's call as a function of set age alone.

How much of the signal that slope is takes two numbers, because they point opposite ways. Ablating the static set-age and rarity features removes only a substantial minority of the all-fold IC (22%), 0.333 to 0.260. The ranking does not collapse without them. But ranking on set age alone, with no model at all, already scores 0.309 (93% of the full-feature IC) and the model's out-of-fold ranking correlates 0.374 with set age. The features are heavily redundant with the lifecycle, which is exactly why removing the explicit ones costs so little: the rest re-encode it. Read the ranking as a set-lifecycle ordering first (old sets drifting one way, new sets the other) and as a trading signal second. See the Models and Methods pages for the exact ablation numbers and the current verdict; the correlation printed on the chart is computed live from the current set medians.

4 · Conformal interval

A point prediction needs an honest error bar. intervals.calibrate_slice_bands collects out-of-fold residuals (y − pred) from the evaluation folds only (never the selection folds, which were already used to pick the model) and takes their empirical 10th and 90th percentiles (plain numpy.quantile, hand-checkable) within each rarity tier. A tier with fewer than 200 residuals borrows the pooled band instead of being handed a noisy one of its own. Tier widths run from 0.267 to 0.490, a 1.8× range that a single band used to average away.

Coverage is measured out of the calibration sample: interval_eval.compute_honest_coverage recalibrates each held-out fold on strictly prior folds and scores that fold forward. The number this page shows (82.1% against an 80% target) is that measurement. The older published figure scored the calibration sample against its own quantiles, which returns 80% for literally any predictor (noise included) and measured nothing.

Does the modelling earn the band? Scored the same way, quoting the unconditional distribution of past returns with no model at all needs width 0.449 for 82.4% coverage, against the model's 0.425 at 82.1%. That gap (a few per cent of width) is the whole measurable return on the apparatus, and it is published rather than implied.

Even so, this is not a guarantee. Split conformal's finite-sample validity needs exchangeable draws and a calibration rank that exists; it holds on the thousands of residual rows and fails at the honest effective sample: about eight monthly folds, roughly three non-overlapping 3-month label blocks. Read the band as a calibrated historical-residual band.

5 · The dual-flag rule

A card is flagged only when its whole band clears zero. intervals.interval_direction_flag returns candidate_up when the point prediction is positive AND the lower band lo90 > −0.02, and candidate_down for the mirror case (pred < 0 AND hi90 < +0.02); everything else is none. If a band is unavailable, percentile_direction_flag falls back to the top / bottom decile of the score (outlook.assign_quantile_safe).

Because the tolerance is tight, most cards resolve to none. The rule is deliberately conservative.

6 · Momentum vs reversion

Is there tradeable trend? momentum.per_rebalance_spreads sorts the universe each rebalance month by trailing formation return into five quintiles, holds three months, and records the Q5−Q1 spread. momentum.summarize_spreads averages across rebalances and deflates the t-statistic for the heavy return overlap (an effective sample size, not the naive rebalance count), and momentum.verdict_for labels each strategy.

Pooled over the whole cross-section, no formation window clears the overlap-deflated significance line: form1m -2.9 points (t_eff -1.82, null), form3m -6.4 points (t_eff -1.74, null). The point estimates lean negative, but a lean inside the noise band is not a measured effect, so the pooled study supports neither tradeable momentum nor tradeable reversion.

The pooled sort is not the only way to ask the question, and the report says so itself: STRATIFIED RE-EXAMINATION (read this with the line above): the pooled null is partly a COMPOSITION artifact. Ranking the same cards inside their own era instead of across the whole cross-section turns 4 pooled horizon(s) directional, and reversion clears the sample-size gates in 2 of 3 eras. After the shared-endpoint (bid-ask-bounce) control, the era-level reversion that survives is Sword & Shield only; momentum survives nowhere (0 momentum cells). This is a POST-HOC subgroup finding on one calendar window with no trading costs applied – it is not a tradeable signal and does not overturn the pooled null. Full tables, sample sizes and caveats below. The full pooled-versus-within-era comparison is on the momentum page.

7 · Value-flag backtest

Does the value residual predict returns out of sample? The pokestat.model.backtest loop walks historical snapshots, assigns each card an under / fair / over flag from its as-of-T hedonic residual, tracks the ~90-day forward return (backtest.compute_forward_return), and pools by flag (backtest.summarize_by_group).

The undervalued-minus-overvalued spread is +1.0 points, so the flag points the right way in pooled aggregate. It is small and regime-dependent across snapshots rather than a reliable edge, and it is gross of costs.

Note too that the overvalued cohort is drawn disproportionately from the momentum study's past-winner quintile, so the two studies are not fully independent evidence.

What the model can and cannot tell you

It can

  • Rank the chase universe by a leakage-free as-of-T signal, with every feature known at prediction time.
  • Attach an honest 80% band and a conservative dual flag, calibrated on held-out evaluation folds.
  • Say where a card sits relative to its own attributes (the hedonic residual) and its own recent price path.
  • Report, with receipts, whether short-horizon returns trend or revert (pooled over the whole cross-section it currently finds neither once overlapping holds are deflated).
  • Say whether that ranking works inside one release window: the era-neutralized held-out IC is +0.343 against a pooled +0.371, so the edge survives removing all cross-era contrast.

It cannot

  • Promise the rank is fully tradeable. Ablating the set-age / rarity features removes only a substantial minority of the all-fold IC (22%), so the ranking does not collapse without them, but that is not the same as it being independent of the set-age lifecycle. Ranking on set age alone, with no model, already scores 0.309 of the 0.333 full-feature IC, so the ranking is substantially reproducible from one variable.
  • Reliably beat the market on value: the backtest under-minus-over spread is small and regime-dependent out of sample.
  • Guarantee the band's forward coverage: monthly folds are autocorrelated, breaking conformal exchangeability, and the finite-sample rank condition fails at the honest effective sample size (~8 folds, ~3 non-overlapping label blocks).
  • Replace judgement: treat every call as a starting point for your own research, not advice.
