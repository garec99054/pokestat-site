Hit rates: what the pull-rate studies say

A hit rate is simply how often, on average, a card of a given rarity turns up in a pack. No manufacturer publishes official pull odds, so this page reads published third-party studies instead, each one built from a stated number of real packs opened and counted. For every set below, by rarity, you can see roughly how many packs it takes to see one hit, a range around that number based on how many packs the study opened, and the odds of pulling each of the set's priciest cards. This page only reports those numbers. It offers no recommendation about opening packs or acquiring singles.

Technical wording

For each set covered here, the most recent third-party pull-rate study is read into a per-rarity table: packs needed for one hit, the interval implied by the study's own sample size, hits and the chance of at least one per 36-pack box, alongside the pull odds of the set's priciest cards. These are independent estimates, not manufacturer-published odds, and this page reports them without any recommendation.

Read this before drawing any conclusion

  • These are third-party pull-rate studies, and the pack samples behind them vary: across the sets on this page they range from 1,000 to 8,500 packs.
  • No manufacturer publishes official pull rates for any set; every number here comes from outside counting of real packs.
  • Pull rates can differ by product. A rate measured from mixed retail packs is not guaranteed to hold for a sealed booster box or any other packaging.
  • Every interval on this page assumes the packs a study opened were a simple random sample of the print run, an assumption the studies themselves do not verify.
  • The odds shown for one specific card assume every card in its rarity tier is equally likely to appear, which studies rarely test directly.
  • Prices change daily and are shown as of this build, not as of the study date.
  • Nothing on this page is a recommendation.
