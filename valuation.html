Valuation: what each chase card is worth vs its price

Every card the model covers, priced against what its pull odds and its popularity say it should cost, with a range around that estimate and a cheap or expensive verdict that respects the range. Cheap here means cheap for what the card is today. It does not mean the card will go up: the cards flagged cheap barely beat the ones flagged expensive, and even that small gap came and went with the market.

Technical wording

Every modeled chase card priced against a hedonic fair value from its pull cost and desirability, with an 80 percent interval and an interval-aware above/below-estimate verdict. The verdict describes mispricing versus fundamentals. It is not a return forecast: the pooled below-minus-above-estimate forward spread is small and regime-dependent.

Experimental: these rankings come from models that have not passed a forward test yet. Each month's predictions are saved before prices move and scored after three months. Not a recommendation. Any model return shown here is before costs. Bought at a realistic price and sold after fees, the top 10% returned -24.3% over 3 months against -38.8% for all cards. It beat the average card in 7 of 8 months and made money in 0 of 8.

Fair value comes from a hedonic regression: log(price) ~ log(pull_cost) + desirability (45/45/10 premium/artwork/appeal), leave-one-out R² 0.645. The residual is how far the market sits from that fair value.

The 80% interval mirrors the forward model’s conformal band; a card is flagged only when its price clears the whole band. Out-of-calibration coverage (jackknifed) is 79.5%.

This is a mispricing map, not a buy list. The flags describe value versus fundamentals; backtested out of sample the below-minus-above-estimate forward spread is only +0.8pt and regime-dependent.

Methodology & caveats

The hedonic fair value

Fair value is the point estimate; residual % is how far the market sits from it. A positive residual reads above the model estimate, negative below it.

The 80% interval & verdict

The verdict is interval-aware: the market must clear the whole band, not just the point, to be flagged. We publish coverage_loo (the jackknifed out-of-sample estimate); the naive in-sample number is circular and labelled as such.

Hierarchical cross-check

Disagreement between the pooled OLS verdict and the per-set Bayesian one is surfaced, not hidden: it flags cards whose set context pulls fair value away from the pooled fit.

Persistence & combined signals

Rank persistence is not return persistence; the combined signals are descriptive pairings of the interval verdict with a recent price move (the momentum study finds no significant short-horizon effect), not a forecast of any single card.

Read this before treating any flag as a buy or sell

  • The flags describe mispricing, not future returns. Backtested at 7 historical snapshots with leakage-free as-of-T features, the pooled below-minus-above-estimate forward spread is only about and is regime-dependent (2024 negative: 0 of 2 snapshots positive, mean -1.2 points; 2025 mixed: 2 of 4 snapshots positive, mean -1.4 points; 2026 positive: 2 of 2 snapshots positive, mean +6.5 points). An above/below-estimate flag has NOT predicted returns out of sample.
  • Persistence is descriptive. A card can stay in the above-estimate tail for snapshots on end without being shown to underperform; see the spread above.
  • Two price sources by design. The fit uses the live market snapshot; trailing returns use the canonical monthly panel (), so the two deliberately differ.
  • Published-number disclosure. The hedonic universe now covers all modelable cards (complete desirability), superseding the stale 135-card vintage; the fair-value fit, the hierarchical cross-check and the backtest now share one coherent universe.
Momentum and reversion: no tradeable momentum, one era-stratified horizon shows reversion

Is this hot? Momentum and reversion

A mispricing flag above answers "is this priced right for what it is." It says nothing about whether the price has been moving. The study below asks that second, separate question directly: does a recent price move predict what happens next. The pooled answer is no tradeable momentum, and the second half re-examines that within each era.

Momentum and short-horizon reversion

Significance uses an overlap-deflated effective sample size, not the naive fold count.

Each rebalance the universe is sorted into quintiles by trailing formation return and held three months. A positive winners-minus-losers spread would mean momentum; a negative one means reversion.

Short horizons: past 1-month winners underperformed losers by 2.5 points (t_eff -1.5, null) and past 3-month winners by 6.4 points (t_eff -1.8, null). Neither clears the overlap-deflated significance line, so these are point estimates inside the noise band, not measured effects.

Practically, there is nothing here to trade on in either direction: the pooled data does not support chasing recent winners or fading them.

The same cards, ranked inside their own era

The table above sorts all modeled cards into quintiles against each other, so a card is partly sorted on which era it belongs to rather than on its return against its own peers. The blocked estimator changes exactly one thing: quintiles are formed within each era and the per-era spreads are averaged. Same universe, same rebalance dates, same overlap-deflated significance machinery.

A pooled null has two very different explanations: no effect anywhere, or an effect inside each stratum that pooling washes out. Pooling eras that sit in different lifecycle phases means the pooled quintile sort partly sorts cards on era membership rather than on their return relative to their own peers.

At the 1-month window the pooled spread is -2.5 points (t_eff -1.46, no signal); ranking the same cards inside their own era gives -5.1 points (t_eff -5.11, reversion). Nothing about the cards, the dates or the significance machinery changed, only which field each card is ranked against.

Read the verdict words carefully. no signal means a measurable spread indistinguishable from zero. insufficient means there was not enough independent data to distinguish anything at all, which is a different statement and is never counted as a null.

Not independent of the value backtest. The value backtest tags a card "above model estimate" when its market price sits well above the forward model's fair value, and those above-estimate cards are drawn disproportionately from this study's top (past-winner) quintile, markedly so at the longer 6-month formation window, because a recent run-up is exactly what lifts market above fair value. So the two studies re-test overlapping recent-run-up cards rather than independent samples. Neither shows a significant pooled effect: the pooled quintile spreads here are within noise once the overlapping holds are deflated, and the backtest's under-minus-over spread is small and regime-dependent. Read them as two lenses on the same population, not as independent confirmations. The same warning applies to the stratified cells above, which are cut from that one panel over that one calendar window.
