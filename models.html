Which model was selected, and why

Candidate models and the three baselines that must be beaten (a zero prediction, a momentum-only score, and a reversion-only score) over the walk-forward folds. The selected model is graded honestly on disjoint held-out evaluation folds, then broken down to show whether its edge is skill inside one release window or merely the eras ranked against each other.

Model leaderboard

The all-fold mean IC and information ratio are the selection-window statistic; the selected model is graded on the disjoint held-out evaluation folds, called out below the table. Coverage is the split-conformal 80 percent interval hit rate on the eval residuals.

The information coefficient (IC) is the cross-sectional rank correlation between predicted and realized returns each fold; higher is better, and it is deflated for fold autocorrelation via an effective sample size.

rf was chosen on the selection folds (mean IC 0.283) and graded on disjoint held-out folds, scoring mean IC 0.368 at information ratio 10.21.

How much of that is the set-age lifecycle? Two different statistics answer that and they point opposite ways, so both are given. Ablating the static set-age and rarity features moves all-fold mean IC from 0.328 to 0.260, removing a substantial minority of the signal (21%), so the ranking does not collapse without them. It is still substantially a lifecycle ordering, though: ranking on set age alone with no model at all already scores 0.296, 90% of the full-feature IC, and the model's out-of-fold ranking correlates 0.370 (Spearman) with set age. The ablated residual clears the noise floor on held-out folds, so a non-static edge survives — but a ranking this reproducible from one variable should be read as a lifecycle map first and a trading signal second.

Recovered listing-book features: what they bought, and what they really are

tcgcsv returns five price points for every product — lowPrice, midPrice, highPrice, marketPrice, directLowPrice. The panel used to keep only marketPrice and discard the rest at aggregation time. They describe the shape of the listing book around the traded price — width, where trades clear inside it, ask-side overhang, dealer inventory — which is a liquidity and dealer-pressure proxy.

Six scale-free features are derived from them, all normalised by market and all read from the date-T snapshot (and the T−3m snapshot for the two deltas), so they are strictly as-of-T: rel_spread, market_pos, mid_skew, direct_discount, d_rel_spread_3m, d_market_pos_3m. They are populated across all 29 historical monthly snapshots (low 100%, mid 100%, high 100%, direct_low 42%), which is what makes them usable as training features at all rather than a present-day snapshot leaking backwards.

Held-out mean rank IC moved 0.313 → 0.368 (+0.055). The static-ablated held-out IC moved 0.233 → 0.285, and the selection folds chose the same model (rf) on both arms, so the comparison is not confounded by a change of learner: same panel, same folds, same selection and evaluation split, only the feature list differs. But read the size against the error bars. State it plainly: the move (+0.055) is SMALLER than the 'before' arm's own one-SE band (0.083). On these error bars a reader cannot conclude that the improvement clears sampling noise, and no joint significance test of the two arms is claimed anywhere in this section.

Where the gain comes from, and what it really is

The gain is concentrated, not spread across the six. Adding mid_skew alone to the pre-spread feature set already reaches held-out 0.364, against 0.313 without it and 0.368 with all six. The other five contribute the remainder.

What that decay means, stated rather than buried. That is the signature of a mechanical catch-up, not of demand forecasting: TCGplayer's `market` is a trailing transaction-weighted average, while `mid` is the CURRENT listing midpoint, so `mid` carries fresher information than `market` at the same instant. A large part of this feature's edge is therefore the stale average converging on a book that was already visible at T -- honest as-of-T signal, but substantially a measurement-lag artifact of how the price series is constructed rather than a forecast of future demand. A buyer paying live listing prices would capture less of it than the IC suggests.

This is disclosed rather than buried because it changes how the improvement should be read: the recovered fields are legitimate, leakage-free, as-of-T data from an already-approved source, and the IC gain is real on held-out folds — but it is mostly one feature, and that feature is substantially exploiting a lag inside the price series rather than predicting the market.

Is that edge within-era skill, or just cross-era contrast?

A pooled cross-sectional IC can be earned two ways: by ranking cards correctly inside one release window, which is the decision a buyer actually faces, or merely by ranking the eras against each other. Both tables re-score the same held-out folds and the same out-of-fold predictions as the headline; only which rows enter each correlation changes.

Two rows do the work. Within-era neutralized re-ranks every card against only its same-era peers, so any skill that comes purely from putting one era above another is removed; it scores +0.355. Between-era contrast only does the opposite, destroying every within-era distinction and keeping just the era ordering; it scores +0.144.

If the pooled number survived neutralization it is skill you could use inside one release window. If instead it lived in the contrast row, the model would be saying little about which card to prefer among cards released together, which is the choice a buyer actually makes.

A thin badge means the row clears its noise bar but on too few effectively independent folds to be counted as evidence by this repo’s own gate. It is shown, not hidden, and it is not treated as corroboration.
