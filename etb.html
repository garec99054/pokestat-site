Elite Trainer Boxes: did they go up, and is one worth holding?

Every Elite Trainer Box in the price archive, measured over 31 monthly snapshots from March 2024 to September 2026. Prices went up a lot. Nearly every explanation of why, and every guess about what comes next, falls apart once it is checked: these months are one long boom with no crash anywhere inside them, so there is no fall here to learn from. This page measures what happened. It is not investment advice.

Technical wording

Every Elite Trainer Box in the price archive, measured over 31 monthly snapshots from March 2024 to September 2026. Prices rose a lot. Almost every interesting claim about why, and every claim about what happens next, turns out not to survive the window: this is one historic sealed boom with no bust in it anywhere. This page is measurement, not investment advice.

Read this before any number on this page

The archive is 31 monthly observations, 2024-03 to 2026-09 – about 2.5 years of a single regime, a historic Pokemon sealed boom. The deepest peak-to-trough fall anywhere in it is -3.4%. There is no bust in this data, so nothing here measures what happens in one, and no interval on this page is a forecast interval: the confidence bands are sampling uncertainty inside the boom. Statistical uncertainty here is small; regime uncertainty is everything.

It does not go back further because it cannot. The tcgcsv price archive begins in 2024-03, and the sources that would extend it are excluded on terms-of-service grounds. “The last few years” means 2.5 years.

Did ETB prices go up?

Yes, sharply. The chart puts every defensible construction of the same index on one axis, with the broader market beside it, because the constructions disagree by more than 30 points a year.

Over 2024-03 to 2026-09 the ETB index ran to 319.2, a multiple of 3.19x, or 59.1%/yr with a HAC 95% interval of [22.2%, 107.2%] (t = 3.45 on 30 monthly log returns, 3 Newey-West lags). That single figure is a construction choice: the 15 defensible ways to build the same index span 58.8% to 90.5%/yr, 31.7 points apart, with a median of 81.7%. The higher numbers come from the constant basket, which by construction can only hold the 62 products that already existed in 2024-03 AND survived to 2026-09. That is a survivor-cohort number, not a market. The deepest peak-to-trough fall anywhere in the archive is -3.4% (2025-11 to 2026-01), which is how little downside this window contains.

Did ETBs beat the alternatives?

The difference is estimated on the paired monthly return difference, so the common market tide cancels before the standard error is formed.

Not one of the 3 benchmark differences clear the |t| >= 2 bar. Against booster boxes over the same window and the same construction the ETB edge is -0.3pp (t = -0.03): statistically indistinguishable. The +13.8pp edge over singles carries t = 1.04, which is not established. So “ETBs went up 59%/yr” is mostly “the Pokemon market went up”. The decision-relevant question was never whether they rose but whether they beat the alternative, and on this archive that is unproven.

What the average hides

Every product with enough history to annualise, drawn individually. The spread between them is the point, not the median.

The median product grew 63.9%/yr, but the middle of the distribution spans 40.5% to 91.7%/yr, an interquartile range of 51 points, and the whole range runs -54.4% to 187.0%/yr. 17 of the 108 products are down over their own observed window (16%), and 67 have growth that clears |t| >= 2 on their own error bar. Picking one box is not the same bet as owning the index.

Those are counts of SKUs, and SKUs cluster inside sets: one release can ship a regular box, a Pokemon Center box and artwork variants whose prices move together because they are the same cardboard. These 108 products are only 60 release events (1.80 SKUs per set), so read any count above as at most that many independent confirmations. Collapsing to one observation per set moves the index only from 59.1%/yr to 65.7%/yr, so the equal-weight-by-SKU choice is not what produced the headline. What does move is the floor: the worst single SKU grew 11.6%/yr while the worst whole SET reached 32.6%/yr, because a set’s weak variant is averaged against its strong ones.

Is it still going up?

The follow-on question to the window above, asked on the window's own last months, with the sample every answer rests on drawn under it.

Yes, on the only reading the archive supports: it has not stopped. Over the trailing 12 months 88 of the 91 ETBs priced at both ends rose (96.7%), the median box ran 1.55x and the index compounded at 41.9%/yr [0, 101] against 59.1% [22, 107] over the full window – intervals that share 92% of the narrower of the two. The formal test of a change puts the recent months -1.59pp/month against the earlier ones with a standard error of 2.04pp (t = -0.78, p = 0.44): no detectable change. On 12 recent and 18 prior returns that is much better evidence against a collapse than against a mild slowdown, and it is a statement about months already observed. Nothing here forecasts the next one.

What this section does and does not establish

  • It is not concentrated. The top 10% of movers (9 products) hold 19.6% of the summed log rise, 1.96x their even share, and the top half hold 71.6%. A rise carried by a handful of products would put those ratios far higher.
  • It is not narrowing. Holding the product set fixed at the 66 boxes priced across both, the share rising went from 97.0% in first 12m to 98.5% in last 12m. The comparison needs a fixed cohort: the products priced in one window are not the products priced in the other.
  • The falls that did happen were shallow. 6 negative months in the whole archive (2024-04 at -1.08%, 2024-05 at -0.61%, 2025-12 at -2.32%, 2026-01 at -1.13%, 2026-08 at -2.07%, 2026-09 at -0.98%), and the deepest peak-to-trough fall inside the trailing 12 months is -3.4%. A drawdown that shallow after a rise this large is not reassurance; it is the clearest sign that no down market has been observed here at all.
  • The exceptions are named. 3 products fell over the trailing window: Mega Evolution Pokemon Center Elite Trainer Box (Exclusive) [Mega Gardevoir] (0.54x), Mega Evolution Pokemon Center Elite Trainer Box (Exclusive) [Mega Lucario] (0.62x), Temporal Forces Pokemon Center Elite Trainer Box (Exclusive) [Walking Wake] (0.95x). Both are Pokemon Center exclusive products, which is where the launch-decay path in the section below lives.
  • The only visible split is channel. Mass-retail ran 31.5% above Pokemon Center exclusive over the window (t = 5.11, 60 against 31 products). Age band and price quartile do not separate: their intervals overlap and their shares of the rise sit within a few points of their shares of the products.

What these numbers are, and are not

  • Popularity is not in this database and is not claimed here. price_history carries price points only: no volume, no sales count, no seller count, no listing quantity. The rankings above are by highest median market price, which is a price level and orders expensive boxes, not wanted ones. A box can be dear because almost nobody lists it.
  • 711 of 1,561 ETB set-months (45.5%) rest on a single priced listing. 25 of the 60 sets have exactly one Elite Trainer Box in the archive and the richest has 4. Where a set-month is one SKU, its series inherits that one listing's idiosyncratic noise whole and no averaging softens it. The booster-box index is worse on the same measure (1,226 of 1,308 rows built on one product), which is why this was checked rather than assumed.
  • The movers window is fixed and stated, and n travels with every row. 2026-03 to 2026-09, both endpoints real observations, at least 5 of 7 months priced. 10 of the 108 boxes do not qualify and are absent rather than estimated. A large fall on a box with few archive months is launch decay measured on a short panel, not a market view.
  • Volatility is a quote series, not a traded one. The market field is a trailing sales average, so these are the wobbles of a published quote. Each box needs 12 consecutive monthly returns before a number is published; 14 boxes fall short and are left out. The typical sampling error is about 4.2 percentage points, so the calmest box (14%/yr) and the wildest (145%/yr) are far apart but neighbours in the middle of the range are not distinguishable.
  • The chase-card correlation is on monthly log RETURNS, never on price levels. Two series that both rise through the same boom correlate near one on levels whatever their real relationship. On returns the median across 51 sets is 0.23 and only 17 of them clear |t| >= 2, so "the box follows the chase card" is a weak tendency and not a rule. The chase card is the set’s dearest card by median price, which is another price proxy for demand.
  • Everything here is inside the same single regime as the rest of the page. One historic sealed boom, no bust anywhere in it. A trailing window drawn from a boom describes a boom.

Age and price

Three looks at one question: whether a box is worth more just for getting older, whether that pattern bends, and whether an older box costs more right now. Open this for the full breakdown, tables and charts included.

Do boxes go up because they age?

This is the question the data cannot answer, and the fact that it cannot is the finding. Both tables below are published so the claim is checkable rather than asserted.

In every row of this panel, age is exactly calendar month minus release month. Regressing age on product and calendar dummies gives R² = 1.000000, and adding age to that design raises its rank by zero (rank deficiency 2). “Do ETBs appreciate as they age” and “did the market rise” are the same regressor over 31 months of one regime. That is not weak identification, it is none. The three standard identifying restrictions each fit every observed price identically, and they imply ten-year ageing multiples from 0.74x to 681x: a 920x span that is pure assumption. Anyone who tells you how much a box appreciates because it aged has chosen one point in that fan.

Two caveats that cut the shape down

  • The 12-month acceleration is the launch cooldown ending, not a property of one-year-old boxes. Drop the launch cohort and it vanishes: -0.120 (t = -0.70).
  • The deceleration is estimated almost entirely from the first calendar half. In the second half it is +0.002 (t = +0.02), indistinguishable from zero.

Do older boxes cost more today?

The owner's own question, asked as a price LEVEL rather than a growth rate: what does an old box sell for right now, and what can and cannot be concluded from that.

Yes -- and it is a vintage ranking, not an ageing curve. At 2026-09, mass-retail ETB price rises with age at Spearman rho = 0.719 (p = 6.9e-12, n = 67): $73 at <1yr against $831 at 8yr+. Pokemon Center exclusives give rho = 0.417 (p = 0.011, n = 36), which is indistinguishable from zero, so this is a mass-retail fact rather than an ETB fact. Following each box's own realised path instead of comparing different vintages at one instant, in-window appreciation depends on the age a box was at entry by +2.1pp/yr per year of age (t = 1.31) – no age dependence distinguishable from zero. It says what a box made in an earlier year costs today. It does not say what a box bought today will cost when it is that old, and this study publishes no forecast.

What this gradient is not

  • The medians do NOT rise monotonically. There are 2 inversions, the worst being 3-5yr -> 5-8yr at -7.2% – older product selling for less than younger product.
  • 0 of those 2 dips separate at the bootstrap interval, and the step up into the oldest band does separate either – despite that one step supplying 84% of the whole top-to-bottom range. The point estimates are what they are; their ORDER is not established by this sample.
  • The oldest band is where the level lives and where the sample is worst. Only 60.0% of the 25 same-era mass-retail products in the catalogue are still priced – 6 were never priced and 4 were delisted – so $831 is a median over SURVIVORS. Putting every missing box at the cheapest and then the dearest observed price bounds the true band median in [$421.79, $1,800.00], a 4.27x span that contains the survivor median rather than being centred on it.
  • The rate question is a different question, and its answer is a null. Whether a box appreciates FASTER because it is old is measured in the two sections above and in the age-at-entry cohorts below; none of those contrasts clears this page's evidence bar. A higher price LEVEL and a higher growth RATE are not the same claim, and only the first of them is supported here. Dropping the products still inside their launch window at entry – whose early decay is launch behaviour rather than ageing – moves the panel's ageing slope to -1.8pp/yr (t = -1.21): no age dependence distinguishable from zero, the opposite sign to an ageing reading of the table above. That cut is a post-hoc slice of one regime, so the headline null is still the statement to quote; it is printed because its sign is decision-relevant, not because it establishes that age is bad for a box.

What would a holder actually have kept?

An index is not money. Sales tax in, marketplace take out and shipping are charged once, which is why the answer to how long to hold is arithmetic.

The frictions are a one-time toll, not a rate, and that single fact answers the question. At the measured gross rate a three-month hold nets -45.8%/yr, a twelve-month hold +23.4%/yr and a twenty-four-month hold +41.1%/yr. To beat cash at three months the market would have to deliver 196%/yr gross; at twenty-four months it only needs 19%/yr. This is arithmetic about a fee schedule and is the one conclusion here that does not depend on the regime. The minimum hold that beats a 4.5% savings account is 8 months on the median box, 9 months on a $124 box and 7 on a $1,081 one, because shipping and the flat processing charge do not scale with the price. An OPTIMAL hold is a different matter and this study declines to name one: the best observed horizon was 18 months at 66.8%/yr net, but the archive contains 1 non-overlapping window of that length. That is one draw, an anecdote, not an estimate.

One box is not the index. At twelve months the middle 50% of single-box outcomes spans about 73 annualised points and 35% of picks did worse than simply owning the basket.
